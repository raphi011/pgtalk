<Note appearAt={1} hideAt={2}>

**One of the 3677, and they all have this shape.** The executor never reads
less than one and never reads more: a page is what comes off the disk, what sits in the buffer cache, and
what `EXPLAIN (BUFFERS)` counts.

</Note>

<Note appearAt={2} hideAt={3}>

**The same 24 bytes at the front of every one**: a WAL position, a checksum,
and the two offsets that say where the free space starts and ends.

</Note>

<Note appearAt={3} hideAt={4}>

**Two arrays, at opposite ends.** Line pointers grow from the front; tuple
data is written from the back. A line pointer is 4 bytes: an offset, a length
and a flag.

The indirection is what lets a tuple move inside its page without anything
outside the page noticing.

</Note>

<Note appearAt={4} hideAt={5}>

**Free space is what is left in the middle.** `pd_lower` and `pd_upper` in the
header are those two boundaries, and the gap between them is the page's
capacity for the next row.

</Note>

<Note appearAt={5} hideAt={6}>

**They meet, and the page is full.** A row that does not fit goes on another
page. Nothing is ever split across two pages — which is why a row larger than
about 2 kB needs the TOAST mechanism at the end of this session.

</Note>

<Note appearAt={6}>

**So read the header of a real one.** `pd_lower` and `pd_upper` are the two
numbers the diagram just drew.

</Note>

<Runnable
  appearAt={7}
  sql={`SELECT lower, upper, special, pagesize, lower - 24 AS lp_bytes, upper - lower AS free
FROM page_header(get_raw_page('orders', 0));`}
/>
