<Note appearAt={1} hideAt={2}>

**Statistics, from a sample.** `ANALYZE` reads about 30,000 rows per table,
whatever the table's size, and keeps summaries: the most common values, a
histogram of the rest, the fraction of NULLs.

</Note>

<Note appearAt={2} hideAt={3}>

**Cost constants.** `seq_page_cost = 1.0`, `random_page_cost = 4.0`,
`cpu_tuple_cost = 0.01`. They are relative: everything is priced in multiples
of one sequential page read.

</Note>

<Note appearAt={3} hideAt={4}>

**Candidate plans.** Every access path for every table, every join order,
every join algorithm. Up to twelve tables the search is exhaustive.

</Note>

<Note appearAt={4} hideAt={5}>

**Arithmetic.** Each candidate is priced by multiplying estimated pages and
rows by those constants.

</Note>

<Note appearAt={5} hideAt={6}>

**No I/O, no sampling at plan time.** Planning a query against 500 million
rows takes about as long as planning it against an empty table.

</Note>

<Note appearAt={6}>

**The cheapest wins.** When the chosen plan is bad, one of the three inputs
was stale, missing or wrong. The planner did its job correctly on bad inputs.

</Note>
